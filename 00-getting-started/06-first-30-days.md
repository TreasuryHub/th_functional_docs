# Your first 30 days: quick wins

> **Go live in the order that pays back fastest**
>
> **Why it matters**
> - **Each step stands on its own**: every milestone delivers something usable before the next one starts.
> - **Nothing is replaced**: your banks, ERP and ledgers stay where they are while this is switched on around them.

> **Who this is for:** whoever owns the rollout, and anyone who wants to know what lands when.

## Overview
Weeks to go live, without replacing your ERP or the rest of your stack. These six milestones are
the order most teams follow, and each one is worth having even if you stop there.

## 1 · Connect the banks
Bring every account into one feed — SWIFT, Open Banking, APIs, files, email or SFTP, whichever each
bank supports.

**What it gives you:** balances and movements arriving on their own, with no portal logins and no
manual downloads.

- [Integrations: Overview](../02-integrations/overview.md) · [Open Banking (PSD2)](../02-integrations/open-banking.md) · [SWIFT Connectivity](../02-integrations/swift.md)

## 2 · See the consolidated cash position
With the feeds running, the Home screen shows every bank, currency and entity in one figure you can
stand behind.

**What it gives you:** the "how much cash do we have?" question answered the moment you log in,
instead of at the end of a spreadsheet exercise.

- [Home: Cash Position](../01-home/home-dashboard.md) · [Bank Accounts](../06-reporting/bank-accounts.md) · [Ingestion Activity](../02-integrations/ingestion-activity.md)

## 3 · Put your signing policy in place
Set approval rules by amount, entity, currency and account before anyone pays from the platform.

**What it gives you:** four-eyes control and a full audit trail from the first payment, rather than
retro-fitted later.

- [Payment Approval Rules](../10-admin-console/payment-approval-rules.md) · [Users & Roles](../10-admin-console/users-and-roles.md) · [Counterparties & SSIs](../10-admin-console/counterparties-and-ssis.md)

## 4 · Automate the first reconciliation
Pick the reconciliation that costs your team the most time — usually bank against ledger, or a PSP
against settlement — and model it once.

**What it gives you:** most items matched automatically from then on, with your team handling only
the exceptions.

- [Reconciliation: Overview](../04-reconciliation/overview.md) · [Workflows](../04-reconciliation/workflows.md) · [Rules & Criteria](../04-reconciliation/rules-and-criteria.md)

## 5 · Produce the first forecast
Once movements are flowing and categorized, the forecast builds on data you already have.

**What it gives you:** funding gaps visible weeks ahead, and a number you can take to the board.

- [Cash Forecast](../06-reporting/cash-forecast.md) · [Transaction Tagging](../02-integrations/transaction-tagging.md) · [Category Positions](../06-reporting/category-positions.md)

## 6 · Hand the routine to an agent
By now you have done each task once and turned it into a workflow. The third step is to let an
agent run it on a schedule or on an event, with approval on every action it takes.

**What it gives you:** the recurring work runs without you, and you review rather than execute.

- [AI Assistance: Overview](../09-agents/overview.md) · [Working with an Agent](../09-agents/assistant.md) · [Alerts](../08-alerts/alerts.md)

## Tips to get more value
- Do the milestones in order. Each one feeds the next: the forecast is only as good as the
  categorization, and the categorization is only as good as the feed.
- Switch on one bank end to end before connecting the rest, so you find the format questions on one
  account rather than twenty.
- Agree the approval policy before the first payment, not after — it is far easier to start with
  the control than to add it to a running process.
